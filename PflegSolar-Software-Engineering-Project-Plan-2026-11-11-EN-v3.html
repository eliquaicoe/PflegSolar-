The owner and cleaner journey

How the service works

StepWhat happensWhen something prevents progress
1 · JoinOwners and cleaners create profiles. The owner adds each installation and its details.Incomplete cleaner profiles stay inactive.
2 · Provide dataUpload history, connect supported MQTT equipment, or use both.Flag missing or invalid readings; keep source, time and units.
3 · AssessCheck cleaning evidence and expected extra electricity value against cleaning and travel costs.No automatic invitation when evidence or positive benefit is missing.
4 · Get offersInvite suitable cleaners automatically. Cleaners quote or decline.No offers, or all rejected: widen the search within agreed limits.
5 · BookThe owner compares the final offers and accepts one.Save one booking and close other invitations. Cancellation stops the search.
6 · CompleteThe cleaner reports the actual cleaning date; the owner confirms the work.Unconfirmed or disputed work stays unresolved.

Whole-service goal: 99.9% a year

About 8 h 46 min downtime in a 365-day year. Measure the complete dashboard and booking journey; component targets alone do not establish this result. This is a proposed goal.

The panel connection goes offline

Panel connection / reading collector
  • Power generation remains independent of our website.
  • Show the last reading with its time; mark it out of date.
  • Reconnect; missing readings are not zero production.

Automatic analysis stops

Analysis application
  • Keep earlier advice visible with its date.
  • Retry when the service recovers.
  • Send no automatic request without enough evidence.

Saved information is unavailable

Database / website service
  • Explain that booking is temporarily unavailable.
  • Keep backups and practise restoring them.
  • Show success only after a booking has been saved.

A booking is sent twice

Website service
  • Check who is allowed to accept the offer.
  • Accept only one cleaner for the same job.
  • Handle retries without creating a second booking.

Protect records; keep the setup manageable

Protect bookings and history with backups and tested recovery. Charts and advice can wait briefly. One application server is a possible simple start, with a shared outage risk. Supabase remains a separate dependency. Extra running copies add cost and upkeep; hosting is still to be agreed.

Understanding and finding failures

Logs, monitoring and troubleshooting

Logging

Record what happened
  • MQTT connection lost or restored
  • Reading rejected and why
  • Cleaning advice and its reason
  • Request, offer or booking changes
  • Failed sign-in or denied access

Monitoring

Watch whether the service works
  • Can users open their dashboard?
  • Can bookings be saved?
  • Are live MQTT readings arriving?
  • Did the analysis job fail?
  • Alert support when action is needed.

Troubleshooting

Find the failed step and recover
  • MQTT readings missing? Check logs.
  • Find the failed connection or step.
  • Restore it; check readings return.
  • Follow a job using its request ID.

One event record

JSON · fixed fields + grouped details
  • Time in UTC, importance, component and event.
  • Outcome, reason or error; site/request IDs where relevant.
  • Keep event-specific details together in a nested JSON object.

Keep these out of logs

Private information and raw measurement streams
  • Passwords, tokens, access keys, raw photos and full addresses.
  • Keep electricity measurements in the measurement store.
  • Logs record events about those readings, not every raw value.

How we developed the design

We first made paper sketches and discussed the system’s components and processes during our three classes and team meetings. We then gave our sketches and discussion notes to AI to help organise the design and suggest corrections. We considered those suggestions, checked them against our project idea and class materials, and applied the changes we agreed were useful. This document brings that work together for classmates to review.
